Why does a longer pendulum swing more slowly?

A small weight hangs from a string. Pull it gently to one side and let go. The motion feels simple, but a surprising relationship is hiding in that arc.

For a simple pendulum at small angles, the period is proportional to the square root of its length.

Doubling the length does not double the time for a swing. It increases the period by a factor of √2. The ideal small-angle model is T = 2π√(L/g), with g approximately 9.81 m/s² near Earth's surface.
